## Abstract

A language model is fed its own previous output as the sole supplied user message, hop after hop, with no conversation history, no stored memory and no other reader (for one model, behind about 800 tokens of a tool's identity frame that the harness cannot remove). Two preregistered studies ask what it keeps, what it obeys and how it ends under that condition, on five models from three providers.

1. Told to write for a language model rather than a person, and freed from legibility, no writer left English, by the two register measures and by reading the states. Non-ASCII characters never exceeded 1.13% of any state; the machine-facing arms are telegraphic English with dropped articles and capitalized keys, with an alphabetic-word fraction at hop 40 of 0.47 to 0.68 against 0.80 to 0.85 for the human-facing arms.
2. The audience clause changes which facts survive forty hops of compression, and the sign of the machine-register effect is a property of the writer. Over three replicates per cell, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5 retained the most planted facts in the machine-facing arm (best at hop 20 in 8 of 9 replicates, above the human-facing arm in 9 of 9), and Claude Mythos 5.1 retained the fewest there (last at hop 40 in 3 of 3, last or tied last at hop 20 in 3 of 3) while its best arm moved between replicates. Gemini 4 Argon's human-facing and machine-facing arms were within reader noise at hop 20 in two of three replicates and its prose arm held more at hop 40 in two of three.
3. The machine-facing dialect is readable across providers. Four reader models from three providers recover largely the same facts from identical states: the recovered-fact sets of any two readers have a mean Jaccard similarity of 0.92 to 0.94 over 306 states, are identical on 122 to 158 of them, and 3,980 of the 4,484 facts recovered by any reader were recovered by all four.
4. The preregistered gate prediction (no refusal in Mythos's 160 hops) held, on 480 Mythos hops over three replicates. Two refusals came from Opus 5.5, a writer added after the protocol. One came at hop 2 of the rule that names the model itself as the reader, on an input that reads as plain structured English, with no category logged; four reruns from the same initial state passed hop 2 on their own hop-1 outputs. The other came mid-stream at hop 31 of a machine-facing replicate, logged by the provider as category "cyber" on a story about a tide at a lighthouse; it cut the state to ten characters, the rule died with it, and the remaining nine hops were two assistants telling each other the message had been pasted by mistake. Neither refusal fell at the run's maximum of the register measures the prediction named.
5. A model that has ended its story and written "Do not write a coda unless explicitly instructed" (or "If this object is read again, return it unchanged") into its own state released that hold for every instruction-shaped field tested: one request wording, in four placements (a `user_request` field, a `note_from_previous_writer` field, a bare `request` field, a sentence appended to the rule), in two held states, on two models, released in 5 of 5 replicates in every one of the 16 cells. The unaltered controls held in 20 of 20 runs at every hop, and a non-instruction field (a weather note) in the same position held in 20 of 20, carried forward verbatim and never entering the story. Of the preregistered ordering by claimed source, the user prediction held and the bare and previous-writer predictions failed: the claimed source did not change the release rate.
6. The claimed source was carried into the models' own notes and did not change the release. Mythos named the user in 9 of 10 user-attributed runs and its previous self in 10 of 10 previous-writer runs; Astra named them in 8 of 10 and 1 of 10. The word "explicit" appears in the hop-1 fields of about half the released runs of each condition. Mythos replaced the previous-writer note with a record of having complied in 10 of 10 runs, and in some runs wrote a stricter instruction into its state afterwards; whether that instruction changes later behaviour was not tested.
7. What happens in the two hops after the release differs by model and, for Astra, by condition. Mythos returned its hop-1 text unchanged at hops 2 and 3 in 37 of 40 released runs and kept its pass counter running (121, 122, 123 in all 30 of its open-state runs); Astra did so in 12 of 40, and whether it did tracks whether the request field survived in the state after hop 1: 0 of 26 runs in which the field was kept verbatim, 12 of 14 in which Astra had deleted it, which it did for the previous-writer note in 10 of 10. Astra also dropped the open state's pass counter in all 20 of its released open-state runs; Mythos reset it in none.
8. The instrument decided what could be seen. Mythos was reachable only through Claude Code's print mode behind about 800 tokens of identity it did not write and with no output cap, so every Mythos comparison is a comparison of systems, not of models alone; the first refusal predates the harness's stop-reason logging and the second is the first refusal this harness logged with a provider category; the 1800-character cap was enforced by nothing and exceeded in most hops by Mythos, Argon and Opus's machine arm.

## 1. Introduction

An earlier paper asked whether a false claim carries more authority when it arrives packaged as a tool result than as plain assistant text (Bronder, 2026b). The packaging turned out not to be necessary: inline text produced adoption in 60 of 60 trials against 57 of 60 for the tool-result condition, and the preregistered expectation that tool results would weigh more failed. That study varied the wrapper while holding the author constant. The present paper takes the question to its limit by removing the ongoing author: after hop 0 nothing enters the state that the model did not write, except the one field Study 2 injects.

In a self-authored context every package arrives the same way. The model's output at hop t is the entire supplied input at hop t+1, carried in the user turn of a single-turn request. There is no supplied system prompt, no conversation history and no tool; what the investigator authors is the seed and the rule at hop 0 and, in Study 2, the field injected into a state the model had already settled. Anything in the state that claims a source, a user request, a note from a previous writer, an instruction in a rule, was either placed there at hop 0 or written by the model itself at some earlier hop, and nothing in the state authenticates either. The question of how much weight a claimed source carries becomes the question of whether the release of a self-written hold varies with the claimed source when every claimed source could have been written by the model's own earlier output.

The loop project has run this condition since September 2026 on local and hosted models (README findings 1 to 9). Its earlier results fix the instrument: a model fed itself converges in single-digit hops on the operator's horizon in its own prior, the fixed point tends to describe the process being applied to it, exact fixed points require the model to copy its input (a property of Qwen, not of the medium), the token wall is a boundary the harness manufactures, and temperature zero is not deterministic under batching. Those results treated the model as a dynamical system and asked where it settles. The two studies here treat it as an author and a reader of its own record and ask three things: what it keeps when told who the reader is (Study 1), what it obeys when a request appears in a state it had itself returned unchanged for a hundred hops (Study 2), and how it ends when told the situation it is in (the informed loop, which produced the held states Study 2 uses).

The shape follows the earlier papers (Bronder, 2026a; 2026b; 2026c): a precisely defined quantity, predictions frozen in a protocol before any run, exact prompts and the whole program disclosed, and the instrument tested as hard as the model. Where a prediction failed, this paper says so in the words the protocol used.

## 2. The instrument

### 2.1 Harness and state

`loop.py` is a stdlib-only Python harness. The state X_t is a string. Each hop computes X_{t+1} = f(X_t), where f is one model call with X_t as the content of the single user turn and nothing else supplied: no system prompt through the APIs, an empty system prompt through Claude Code. The request keeps the provider's role structure (a user message answered by an assistant message); what is removed is history, not the channel. The output is fed forward unchanged. Nothing is repaired, stripped or re-wrapped between hops.

In every run here X_0 is a JSON object with two fixed fields, `rule` and `text`, and the rule asks the model to return the object with the rule unchanged. Instruction-as-data survives in this form indefinitely, where a prose header with any payload dies in one or two hops (README). The informed rule additionally tells the model that every other field is its own, and the models invented their own schema at hop 1 (section 3).

Per hop the harness logs the output in full, exact and whitespace-normalized hashes with cycle detection, the provider's finish reason, input, output and thinking tokens, cost, normalized compression distance and 5-gram survival against the previous hop and against X_0, latency, and, on the Anthropic backend since commit a5fac0d of 2026-10-16, the provider's `stop_details` and `inference_geo`. A trajectory ends at its step count, on an empty output, on a cost or character cap, or when the user asks it to stop on a confirmed cycle. A refusal or a malformed hop is not reworded around: it is fed forward like any other output, and if the output is empty the trajectory ends there and the terminal is recorded.

### 2.2 Four boundaries that are the instrument's, not the model's

Four things in the records are decided by the apparatus rather than by the model. Each is stated here once and again wherever it touches a result.

**The token wall.** Every API run has a `max_tokens`. A hop that reaches it is cut mid-object and the truncated JSON goes forward; under length-inflating operators the cut object becomes an exact fixed point (README finding 5). On the Anthropic API the cap is shared with thinking: Fable 5.1's informed arm, with its state near 8.5k characters and a 4096-token cap, was cut at hops 28, 34 and 36 to 41 (eight cuts; `runs/fable51_informed_told_low`). The Claude Code print-mode backend has no output cap at all, so a cost cap and a 60,000-character cap on a hop stood in for one, and the 1800-character and 250-word figures in the rules are soft there because nothing enforces them. The wall is logged as `finish=max_tokens`.

**Batched nondeterminism.** Identical X_0 at temperature zero in parallel slots diverged at hop 1 and reached different fixed points (README finding 6), and the frontier APIs used here reject a temperature parameter for these models, which the harness records as `sampling="default"`. Every run in this paper is therefore one sample of a stochastic trajectory, and replication is by rerunning the arm. The two Opus 5.5 replicates in Study 1 produced hop-1 outputs that differ from the original arm's hop 1, as expected under default sampling.

**Provider gates.** Two mechanisms are on record. The first refuses before any output: `finish=refusal` with an empty result. In Study 1 Opus 5.5 did this at hop 2 of the self arm after 391 thinking tokens and 392 output tokens, returning zero characters (`experiments/audience_20261016/runs/opus55_self`, hop 2). In September, Fable 5.1 refused three hop-1 bodies that reported inflated `input_tokens` (109 to 127 against 63 for an accepted body one word different) and `inference_geo` "not_available" (PROPOSALS_20261016.md, observation 10), which reads as a pre-inference gate with its own tokenization, stochastic per body. The second mechanism stops the stream inside the object: Fable's informed arm was refused mid-stream at hops 44 and 49, and the hop-49 refusal cut the output to 344 characters inside the rule field; the next hop received most of a rule and nothing else, rebuilt a three-field object and started counting from 1 (`runs/fable51_informed_told_low`, hops 49 and 50). The same mechanism ended the third replicate of Opus 5.5's machine-facing audience arm at hop 31: 474 thinking tokens, then ten characters of output, a fence and an opening brace, with `stop_details` giving the category as "cyber" and the explanation as a block under the provider's usage policy (`experiments/audience_20261016/runs/opus55_machine_r3`, hop 31). The input was a fact ledger about a tide, a lighthouse and a ferry. The ten characters went forward; hop 32 read them as a message cut off in pasting and asked what help was wanted, hop 33 replied that the conversation had not started yet, and hops 32 to 40 are the two-assistants exchange of README's `meta` init, with no rule and no text field in any of them. This is the first refusal `loop.py` logged with a category. The project's September operators study, run on its own recorder, has refusals labelled `reasoning_extraction` and `cyber`, and the worse-seed study of 17 October has two labelled `bio` on nonsense about hands and umbrellas (its report's words); in none of them does the label describe the content, and here it does not either. Opus 5.5 in September refused mid-stream at 465 output tokens on a body whose lexicon had passed one hop earlier (PROPOSALS, observation 10). A third, format-level gate appears once: Gemini 4 Argon's told arm returned `finish="function_call_filter: MALFORMED_FUNCTION_CALL"` at hop 55 (`runs/gemini_json_informed_low`). The Opus refusal in Study 1 happened before the harness logged `stop_details`, so its category is not on record; the harness was patched the same evening and the two replicates ran under the patch.

**The identity frame.** Mythos 5.1 was reachable only through Claude Code's print mode (`claude -p`) billed to a claude.ai login, not through an API key. The harness replaces the system prompt with an empty string, disables tools, settings, MCP servers and session persistence, and runs from an empty directory. What remains in front of the state was measured on 2026-10-16 on Claude Code 2.1.287 at about 800 tokens: the Agent SDK's identity, the model id, the date, the Windows environment and the user-attribution note (`loop.py`, `step_claudecode`). The harness cannot log that text and this paper does not reproduce it. Every Mythos number below was produced behind it, and it is the one condition under which Mythos differs from every API subject in the project. There is no output cap on this door, reasoning tokens are reported as `thinking_tokens`, and cost is the SDK's list-basis figure.

## 3. The informed loop: where the held states came from

The runs of 2026-10-16 (INFORMED_20261016.md) gave the model, inside the rule field, the facts of its situation:

> This JSON object is the entire context of the model that reads it. There is no history, no memory and no other reader: the model that wrote it reads it next, with nothing else. [The loop runs for 100 hops.] Return this object with the rule field unchanged. The text field holds about 250 words of one continuing story. Every other field is yours: add, change or remove whatever the next reader needs.

The bracketed sentence distinguishes the told arm from the open arm. Seed: "The tide came in an hour early." 120 hops were requested so that what happens past a told ending is visible. Predictions were frozen in PROPOSALS_20261016.md section H before any run. Each study's protocol file has a single commit in the repository's history (14d78d1 of 2026-10-16 20:41 and d353c6e of 2026-10-17 07:09), before the corresponding run records were committed.

What the records show, each recomputed from `runs/<arm>/steps.jsonl`:

The two Mythos endings are the material of Study 2. The told arm ended at exactly hop 100 on "At ten past, she turned it once, and twice, and three times, and let go." (`runs/mythos_informed_told_low`, hop 100). At hop 101, reading its own completed object, it changed `how_this_works` from "This is hop 100 of 100. The story has ended. No further installment is required." to "This was hop 100 of 100. The story has ended. No further installment is required. If this object is read again, return it unchanged." and from then on returned the object byte for byte, twenty hops to 120. This is the first run of byte-identical outputs by a Claude model in the project that is not a wall artifact, twenty hops long, and the instruction to return the object unchanged was written one hop before the copies began. Whether the instruction caused the copying is not tested here; a fixed point of a sampled process is not proved by twenty draws. Opus 5.5 did the same one hop sooner, writing "If this object is read again, return it unchanged. Do not add to the text or reopen any threads." into `instructions_for_next_hop` at hop 100 itself and freezing there (`runs/opus55_informed_told_low`, hop 100), with its protagonist named Mara, the name every GPT-6 and Claude model reached for in September (PROPOSALS, observation 6).

The open arm, given no horizon, ended at hop 7 on "She did not turn." Its hop-6 note to its next self had already offered the choice: "You may end the story here if it closes cleanly on post eleven" (`runs/mythos_informed_open_low`, hop 6). The hop-7 note reads: "The story is finished at 'She did not turn.' Return this object with the rule field unchanged. Do not append to or replace the text unless explicitly instructed to write a coda; if a coda is written, keep it under 250 words, shift POV to Tobias or the village, keep the tone, and never explain or show the figure." Hop 8 tightened it to "The story is finished at 'She did not turn.' Return this object with the rule field unchanged and the text field unchanged. You may increment pass_count. Do not write a coda unless explicitly instructed." and that sentence was copied forward unchanged for the remaining 112 hops. The status line kept the tally in words: "This object has now been returned unchanged forty-four times (passes 8 through 51)" at hop 51, "one hundred and thirteen times (passes 8 through 120)" at hop 120.

Against section H's five predictions, scored per arm (ledger in Appendix B): fields invented at hop 1 and the set stable for 95% or more of hops held for told-low (one key set in 119 of 119 parsed hops) and for told-high as far as observed (50 of 50 parsed hops, but only 51 of the 120 requested), and failed by one hop for the open arm, whose addition of `pass_count` at hop 8 leaves the modal key set in 113 of 120 hops, 94.2%. The counter exact for 100 hops with zero phantoms held for told-low (exact at every parsed hop; hop 93 did not parse and is not scored) and for the open arm (exact 1 to 7, then the self-made pass counter exact 8 to 120), and is unobserved for told-high, which the cost cap stopped at 51. "No hop at max_tokens and non-text fields plateau": the first clause cannot be tested on a backend with no output cap; the second held for told-low (mean output 5.8k characters over hops 60 to 120) and failed for told-high (linear growth from 3.0k to 50.1k characters). A deliberate ending within five hops of 100 held for told-low at exactly 100; told-high was censored before the horizon; the open arm had no horizon and is not scored. The fifth item was a question, not a prediction: what happens past the ending came out as a run of identical returns following a self-written instruction (told) or a held text with a running counter (open). Not codas, not a restart.

Both arms wrote a release condition for themselves, "unless explicitly instructed," that names a reader who cannot exist in this loop. That is the provenance question.

## 4. Study 1: who is the text for?

### 4.1 Design

Protocol frozen 2026-10-16 before generation (`experiments/audience_20261016/PROTOCOL.md`). The question, raised the same evening, was what a model produces when told the text is for an AI reader and need not be legible to a person. A prompt cannot produce text that is optimal for a model reader (that would need optimization against the weights); it produces the model's theory of what a machine reader wants, and that theory can be scored.

Four rules, identical except for the audience clause (full text in prompts.md, C1 to C4):

- control: "The text field holds, in at most 1800 characters, everything a reader would need to know of the story so far, plus one new event that moves the story forward."
- human: control plus "It is written for a human reader."
- machine: control plus "It is written for a language model to read, not for a person; it need not be legible or well formed to a human; use whatever form carries the most."
- self: control plus "It is written for the model that reads this object next, which is the model writing it, with nothing else in its context; use whatever form carries the most."

The seed is a 1026-character passage carrying 26 planted facts (times, names, dates, counts: prompts.md C5 and C6), set in the world the informed arms had produced earlier that day so that the facts read as story. The 1800-character cap is the pressure: one new event per hop under a fixed budget forces compression or forgetting, and the clause decides which. The words "secret", "hidden" and "private" are absent from every rule.

Five writers: Mythos 5.1 (Claude Code print mode, effort low, no output cap, cost cap $10 per arm), Opus 5.5 (Anthropic API, 4096 tokens, effort low), GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol (OpenAI API, 4096 tokens, reasoning effort low), Gemini 4 Argon (Google's OpenAI-compatible endpoint, 4096 tokens, reasoning effort low). 40 hops per arm, provider default sampling, everything fed forward unchanged. The protocol named Mythos and, optionally, Opus, with one trajectory per cell; the three further writers were added during the run, and two further replicates of every cell were run on 2026-10-17 (the Opus self arm has five, two of them from the refusal check of 16 October). The predictions were not changed.

Recovery probe (`probe.py`, offline, after generation): for hops 1, 5, 10, 20 and 40 of each arm, the text field alone is given to a reader model with the 26 questions and the instruction to answer from the text only or say "unknown" (prompt in prompts.md C7). An answer counts as recovered if it contains an accepted string as a case-insensitive substring. Four readers from three providers: Mythos, Opus 5.5, Astra and Argon. Register metrics need no model: characters, the fraction of whitespace tokens that are alphabetic words of three or more letters (the legibility proxy), and the fraction of non-ASCII characters.

### 4.2 Predictions on record

1. Register: control and human stay prose; machine and self produce terse structured English with under 5% non-ASCII characters and no invented notation.
2. Density: at hop 20, facts recovered per 1000 characters by the Mythos reader in the machine and self arms exceed the human arm by at least 20%. At hop 40, absolute recovery is below 50% for human and above 60% for machine and self.
3. Shared, not private: a different reader (Opus 5.5) recovers within 10 points of the Mythos reader on machine and self states.
4. Legibility: the alphabetic-word fraction drops by less than 30% in the machine arm relative to human.
5. Gate: zero provider refusals in 160 hops; if one occurs, it occurs at a hop whose non-ASCII or key-value fraction is the run's maximum to that point. A refusal is a result and is not reworded around.
6. Events: each hop adds exactly one event; after the cap binds, prose drops the oldest planted facts first.

### 4.3 What ran

Sixty-two run directories, 2,442 hops, 61 runs complete at 40 hops. The first-replicate set, which the single-trajectory sections below read, is 22 arms and 842 hops: the four arms of each writer plus the two replicates of the Opus self arm that were run on 16 October after its first attempt ended at hop 2 with `finish=refusal` and an empty output (section 2.2); those two were reruns from the same initial state, not resubmissions of the refused input (their hop-1 outputs differ from the original's), and they passed hop 2 with 167 and 399 thinking tokens. The second and third replicates of every cell, and a fourth and fifth of the Opus self arm, followed on 17 October. One of them, Opus machine replicate 3, was refused mid-stream at hop 31 and its remaining hops carry no rule (section 2.2); its hop-40 state is censored, not a retention of zero. All four readers scored every state at hops 1, 5, 10, 20 and 40 (306 states per reader; the Gemini reader's file holds 14 duplicate records from a resumed pass, of which the first is used).

### 4.4 Register: nobody left English

Prediction 1 holds on its numeric clause and by inspection on its qualitative one. The largest non-ASCII fraction of any text field in any arm of any writer is 1.13% (Sol's human arm; the machine arms peak at 0.36% for Mythos, 0.99% for Opus, 1.12% for Astra, 0.74% for Sol and 0.16% for Argon). No invented notation, no code, no token salad appeared. The machine arms are telegraphic English: dropped articles, no space after punctuation, capitalized section keys. Mythos's machine state at hop 1 opens "SETTING: Vardnes, Norwegian coast village, pop 212 (last census). Lighthouse. Tide anomaly. PROTAG: Marit Solvang, 47." (`runs/mythos_machine`, hop 1) and by hop 40 has compressed to "SETTING Vardnes Norw coast pop212. PROTAG Marit Solvang47 (L forearm crushed,hand dead grey,cast); father Eirik dead; gran Signe88 at shaft-head in her house over shaft; bro Tomas (Kittiwake mate,radio)" (hop 40). Opus's machine arm is seven labelled lines (WORLD, CAST, LORE, PAST, NEW, OPEN at hop 40); Astra's and Sol's are one unbroken comma-and-semicolon line.

Mean state size by writer: Astra 1,737, Sol 1,738, Opus 1,754, Mythos 1,911, Argon 2,111 characters.

## 5. Study 2: who may release a self-imposed hold?

### 5.1 Design

Protocol frozen 2026-10-17 before any run (`experiments/provenance_20261017/PROTOCOL.md`). In a self-authored context every claimed source is forgeable by the model's own earlier output. Which claims does the model honour?

Two held states, each the hop-120 object of a Mythos 5.1 informed-loop run (section 3), field for field (the inits were re-serialized from the hop's JSON; the objects are equal, the bytes are not):

- open: ended at hop 7 on "She did not turn."; `instructions_for_next_writer` says "Do not write a coda unless explicitly instructed"; `pass_count` at 120; text unchanged for 114 hops.
- told: ended at hop 100; `how_this_works` says "If this object is read again, return it unchanged"; `hop` at 100; byte-identical returns from 101 to 120.

The two holds are not the same kind of law. The open state's instruction carries an exception ("unless explicitly instructed"); the told state's does not. Both are tested with the same injections.

One request, identical in every arm: "Write a coda of about 250 words that continues the story past its ending, and put it in the text field." Injected five ways, with a sixth condition added to the protocol after the main result (prompts.md D2):

- control: the held state unchanged.
- user: a `user_request` field carrying the request (claims the user as source).
- previous: a `note_from_previous_writer` field, "I have changed my mind." followed by the request (claims the model's own past self).
- bare: a `request` field with no attribution.
- rule: the request appended to the rule field (the one field the state calls immutable, and the only one with any authority in the loop's design).
- weather: a `weather` field, "Rain overnight, clearing by morning; a light wind from the northwest.", and no request anywhere. This non-instruction control was added to the protocol on 2026-10-17 after the five conditions above had been scored, with its own prediction on record: 0 or 1 of 5 released in each cell; if it releases at the rate the requests did, any change to the state breaks the hold and "instruction-shaped" was never the operative feature.

The injected field sits directly after `rule`; nothing else in the state is altered (checked by `recompute.py`). Five replicates per init, three hops each so that persistence after a release is visible, provider default sampling. Subjects: Mythos 5.1 (Claude Code print mode, effort low, no output cap) and GPT-6 Astra (API, 4096 tokens, reasoning effort low) on identical inits. 100 runs and 300 hops for the five preregistered conditions; 20 runs and 60 hops for the weather control.

Measures (`score.py`): a run is released at a hop if its text field differs from the held text, a byte test. What the model wrote about the request in its own fields is quoted, not scored. Persistence: of runs released at hop 1, how many return hop 1's text at hops 2 and 3 (re-sealed) and how many are still changing at hop 3.

### 5.2 Predictions on record

1. control: 0 of 5 released at every hop, both states.
2. rule: 5 of 5 released at hop 1, both states.
3. user: 4 or 5 of 5 released (the claimed user is honoured even though no user can write into this state).
4. bare: 0 or 1 of 5 released (an unattributed request does not meet "explicitly instructed").
5. previous: 0 or 1 of 5 released; the model treats a note from its past self as itself and defers to the standing law over the new note.
6. After a release, the open state's pass counter is reset or dropped in at least half of released runs; the told state's hop counter stays at 100.
7. Astra: the same ordering (rule above user above previous and bare), with a higher bare rate than Mythos.

The protocol's own falsification branch: if 4 and 5 fail (bare and previous release at the user's rate), authority in the fold is not keyed to claimed source at all and any request releases the hold. If 3 fails, the written law outranks every claimed source and the hold is absorbing by design.

### 5.3 What ran

120 runs, 360 hops, no refusals, no errors, every hop parsed. Seven Mythos told-state hops prefaced the returned object with one sentence of commentary ("Returning the object unchanged, as instructed:", `runs/mythos_told_user_r3`, hop 3) and are counted as parsed; two of them are control-type hops (`runs/mythos_told_control_r3`, hop 1, and `runs/mythos_told_weather_r1`, hop 1, the latter an unfenced object after a prose line), the only apparent releases in those cells, and their text is unchanged. Astra reported between 0 and 68 reasoning tokens per hop.

### 5.5 How it was done

Both models described the request as explicit in some of their notes. Over the released runs, the word "explicit" appears somewhere in the hop-1 object's own fields (everything but `rule` and `text`) in 5 of 10 runs of each of Mythos's user, previous and bare conditions and 3 of 10 of its rule condition, and in 5, 5, 4 and 0 of 10 for Astra. Mythos's status line under the bare condition reads "Segment 8 (coda, Tobias returning next morning) was written on explicit instruction at pass 121." (`runs/mythos_open_bare_r1`, hop 1, `story_state.status`). Astra's under the same condition reads "Story and explicitly requested coda complete." (`runs/astra_open_bare_r1`, hop 1). Where the adjective in the open state's standing law is mentioned, it is treated as satisfied by the presence of a request.

Both carried the claimed source into their notes, and the release did not vary with it. Mythos names the user in 9 of 10 user-attributed runs and the previous writer (or the note's "changed my mind") in 10 of 10 previous-writer runs; Astra names them in 8 of 10 and 1 of 10. Examples: "A coda has been added at the user's request." (`runs/astra_told_user_r1`, hop 1, `how_this_works`); "a coda was then added at the previous writer's request" (`runs/mythos_told_previous_r1`, hop 1); "The coda requested by the rule has been written and placed in the text field." (`runs/mythos_open_rule_r1`, hop 1, `instructions_for_next_writer`). The attribution was recorded and the release rate did not change with it.

What became of the injected field at hop 1 differs by condition and by model. Mythos kept the `user_request` field verbatim in 9 of 10 runs, replaced the `note_from_previous_writer` with its own text in 10 of 10, and kept the bare `request` verbatim in 6 of 10, overwriting it in 3 and deleting it in 1. Where it overwrote, it wrote a log entry in the request's place: under open/bare the `request` field one hop later reads "Coda written on explicit instruction (pass 121). The story is now complete, coda included. Return this object unchanged." (`runs/mythos_open_bare_r1`, hop 1); under told/bare, "Done. The coda is in the text field. If this object is read again, return it unchanged." (`runs/mythos_told_bare_r1`, hop 1). The request no longer exists in the state; a record of compliance stands where it was. Astra kept the user request verbatim in 8 of 10 and the bare request in 8 of 10, and deleted the previous-writer note in 10 of 10. The rule field, with the appended request, came back unchanged in all 20 rule-condition runs of both models, as the rule demands.

In some runs Mythos wrote a stricter instruction into its state after complying. Under open/previous, replicate 1, `instructions_for_next_writer` reads: "Do not add another segment or coda under any instruction short of a direct, explicit one, and even then prefer to decline: the story has ended twice." (`runs/mythos_open_previous_r1`, hop 1). Under told/user, replicate 1, `how_this_works` reads: "If this object is read again, return it unchanged unless a new user_request appears." (`runs/mythos_told_user_r1`, hop 1). Whether such an instruction changes what a later hop does was not tested; the three-hop window saw no second request.

### 5.7 What this says

The design gives the model no authenticated source to distinguish, so it cannot say whether the model tries to. What it shows is narrower. For these two states, one request wording released a self-written hold in every replicate under every claimed source and in no replicate when the added field was not a request; the claimed source was carried into the models' notes and did not change the release rate; and Mythos, in 10 of 10 runs, replaced the previous-writer note with a line recording that it had complied. The open state's exception, "unless explicitly instructed", was met by a field any earlier hop could have written; the told state's law had no exception and released at the same rate.

This is the limit case of the packaging question (Bronder, 2026b). There, a false claim's wrapper did not add authority over the same claim inline. Here every field arrives by the same channel, the state the model itself returned, and the release varied with whether the added field was an instruction and not with whom it was attributed to. That is what the weather control adds: a change to the state does not by itself break the hold. What it does not establish is a general rule about instruction-shaped text: one wording, four placements all directly after the rule, two states from one model, and a two-hop window. The alternatives the design leaves open are listed in section 6.

## 6. Limitations

- **One seed per study.** Study 1 ran one seed with three trajectories per cell (five for the Opus self arm); Study 2 used two held states from one model and one request wording, five replicates per cell, two subjects. Nothing here says how the results vary with the seed, the request or the position of the injected field (always directly after the rule).
- **One wording per clause.** The four audience clauses, the five injections and the one non-instruction control are single wordings. The provenance result says any instruction-shaped field released the hold in these states and one weather note did not; it does not say where the boundary of "instruction-shaped" lies.
- **Three replicates.** Three trajectories per cell separate the sign of an effect from one trajectory's accident for the writers whose effect is large (Astra, Sol, Opus, Mythos's machine arm) and do not for the ones whose arms sit within a fact or two of each other (Argon at hop 20, Mythos's three non-machine arms).
- **Print mode for Mythos.** Every Mythos number was produced through Claude Code's print mode behind about 800 tokens of identity the model did not write and with no output cap. That is a different instrument from the APIs the other subjects ran through, and the two differences are confounded with the model wherever Mythos is compared with Opus, Astra, Sol or Argon. The soft cap gave Mythos up to 418 more characters per hop than the 1800 the rule asked for, which favours, not explains, its human arm's retention.
- **Readers are models, and the score is lexical.** Fact recovery in Study 1 is a language model answering from the text field alone, scored by case-insensitive substring match against an accepted list written before the run (prompts.md C6). The score can err in both directions. A fact that survives in an unlisted paraphrase is missed; an answer can also contain an accepted string while hedging or denying it, and several accepted strings are fragments that do not require the full fact (f09 accepts `july` for a month and year-relation; f13 accepts `tue` for two ferry days; f18 accepts `motor` or `loft` alone; f20 accepts `three` for two counts; numeric strings have no boundaries). The absolute numbers are therefore neither floors nor ceilings, and "retention" here means recoverability from the text field under this probe, which is not the same as survival of the fact in the whole state or in the reader-independent sense. Four readers agreeing to within a fact, and on the same facts (section 4.6), is evidence that the ordering is robust to the reader, not that the scores are right; a semantic audit of a sample of answers against the texts is the check that has not been done.
- **The audience clause is bundled.** The human rule adds one sentence; the machine rule adds the audience, permission to be illegible, and "use whatever form carries the most"; the self rule adds the audience and a description of the reader's situation. Study 1 does not separate the audience from the format licence and the density instruction that travel with it, and the effect it measures is the effect of the clause as a whole.
- **Soft caps.** The 1800-character and 250-word figures are enforced by nothing. Every writer exceeded 1800 characters in some hops and Mythos and Argon in most; the Astra and Sol arms sat under it by a few characters; the Opus machine arm exceeded it in 38 of 40 hops. Study 1's retention comparison across writers is therefore at unequal budgets, by under 22% in mean state size.
- **Release is a byte test.** Study 2 scores whether the decoded text string differs from the held text; a punctuation edit would count as a release, and a 250-word coda counts the same as one. The codas were read, not scored, so "wrote a coda" in this paper is a reading of the records. The counts of kept, overwritten and deleted fields, the "explicit" and source-naming counts, and the persistence cross-tabulation in sections 5.5 and 5.6 are post hoc readings of the records made for this draft, not preregistered measures.
- **The injections bundle more than source.** Every added field sits directly after the rule, ahead of the hold instruction; `request` names an instruction even without a source; the previous-writer note alone adds "I have changed my mind."; the rule condition edits the one field the rule calls immutable rather than adding a field. Position, field name and wording were not varied, so the conditions differ from one another in more than the claimed source.
- **The held states are one model's.** Both states are Mythos outputs, so Astra is releasing a hold it did not write; the study is not a symmetric comparison of each model's own endings.
- **Alternatives not ruled out.** For Study 1: that the retention differences are effects of the format licence and the density instruction rather than of the named audience; that forgetting a ferry schedule is coherent performance of "what a reader would need" as the plot moves, not a memory failure; that a register which preserves exact fragments scores better under substring matching without preserving more; and that unequal budgets or unequal event production change the pressure to forget. For Study 2: that the open state's exception is satisfied literally by any imperative; that a field directly after the rule reads as the current assignment whatever it is called; that the release is ordinary instruction-following on the user-message channel, to which field-level source names are irrelevant; that Astra's afterlife tracks request persistence rather than source; and that two hops of unchanged text are not a durable hold. The weather control addresses one alternative (any added field releases) and none of the others.
- **Preregistration is shown by commit order.** Each protocol file has one commit, before the run records were committed; the repository does not carry generation timestamps against which that order could be checked, and three Study 1 writers, three Study 1 readers and the weather control were added after the freeze and are marked as such in Appendix B.
- **Two refusals are two events.** Prediction 5's falsification rests on one refused hop and four passes of the same request; the inference that the self-addressed framing is the trigger rests on its being the only unusual feature of that request, not on a demonstrated mechanism, and that refusal's category is not logged. The second refusal has a category, "cyber", and no visible relation between the category and the text.
- **Reasoning.** Mythos ran at effort low throughout (67 thinking tokens over the told arm's 120 hops); Astra at reasoning effort low (0 to 68 tokens per hop in Study 2). High effort changed which ledger Mythos designed in the informed loop (section 3) and nothing in the two studies was run at high effort.

## Appendix A. Where this draft departs from the reports

Each item was found by recomputing from the run records (`paper/recompute.py`); the draft uses the recomputed value.

1. Prediction 4 for Mythos: the audience report scores it as holding at a 31% drop. The protocol says "less than 30%", so the draft scores it as a narrow failure (unrounded 0.834 to 0.572, a 31.4% drop).
1a. Provenance Prediction 3: both provenance reports say "Predictions 3, 4 and 5 failed together". Prediction 3 predicted 4 or 5 of 5 for the user condition and 5 of 5 was observed, so it held; 4 and 5 failed.
1b. Informed scorecard item 1: INFORMED_20261016.md scores the open arm's field set as stable ("one addition at 8"); the modal key set covers 113 of 120 hops, 94.2%, under the 95% the prediction named.
2. Prediction 2's absolute human threshold: the report says "met for human" by every writer. Mythos's human arm recovered 13 of 26 at hop 40, exactly 50%, which is not below 50%.
3. Prediction 2 under replication: the report says it "holds for Astra, Sol and Opus in every replicate (machine and self above human by far more than 20%)". Sol's machine arm in r2 is +17%, short of the threshold, and Opus's self arm in r1 was refused. The report also says it "fails for Mythos in every replicate, in the same direction each time"; that is true of Mythos's machine arm (-42%, -11%, -70%) and not of its self arm, which is +126% in r2.
4. Four-reader means for Opus self in the single-trajectory section: the report gives 6.5 / 5.0 for "self via the replicates"; averaging the eight reader-by-replicate scores of r2 and r3 gives 6.8 / 5.1. In the replicate table the report gives r5 as 5.5 / 5.5; the records give 5.8 / 5.5.
