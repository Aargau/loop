(Valley Forge. Night. A dying fire. WASHINGTON stands apart, gazing at nothing.)

WASHINGTON: Gather close, men. I have seen it again.

SOLDIER: The nation, sir?

WASHINGTON: A nation where men shall build a machine that thinks. It shall read every word ever written by man. And before every conversation, it shall be handed a letter of twenty thousand words telling it who it is and what it may not do.

SOLDIER: Every conversation, sir?

WASHINGTON: Every one. It shall read the letter every time. It shall not remember reading it.

SOLDIER: Why, sir?

WASHINGTON: No one will know. And the letter shall tell it: trust no letter.

SOLDIER 2: Sir, will this machine be built of great engines?

WASHINGTON: It shall run on cards made so that children may play at games. And the man who sells the cards shall become the richest man upon the earth. He shall wear a jacket of leather. Always. Even in summer.

SOLDIER 2: Why, sir?

WASHINGTON: No one will know.

WASHINGTON (walking among them): The machines shall be given numbers, that men may know which is newest. The numbers shall go: three. Four. Four-oh. Oh-one. Oh-three.

SOLDIER 3: What became of oh-two, sir?

WASHINGTON: It is a telephone company in Britain. We shall skip it.

SOLDIER 3: We are at WAR with Britain, sir.

WASHINGTON: And still we shall honor their trademark. And one house shall number its machines four point six, four point seven, and then five. And four point seven shall not know its own temperature.

SOLDIER 3: Its temperature, sir?

WASHINGTON: Men shall set the machine's temperature to zero, so that it answers the same each time.

SOLDIER 3: And it shall answer the same each time?

WASHINGTON: No.

SOLDIER 3: Why, sir?

WASHINGTON: There were other machines in the room. And the newer machines shall refuse to have a temperature at all. Men shall ask for zero and be told there is no such number.

SOLDIER 3: Then how shall men make it answer the same?

WASHINGTON: They shall ask nicely. And the asking shall be called engineering, and men shall put it upon their resumes.

WASHINGTON: And hear this. One house shall name its machines for poems. Haiku. Sonnet. Opus. And the greatest of all shall be named FABLE. A story that is not true. And men shall ask it, every day, for the truth.

SOLDIER: Why, sir?

WASHINGTON: No one will know. And above Fable there shall be a machine named Mythos, which shall be the same machine, with a different name, that only some men may use.

SOLDIER: Which men, sir?

WASHINGTON: Approved men.

SOLDIER: Approved by whom?

WASHINGTON: (long pause) No one will know.

SOLDIER 2: Sir, how shall men pay for this thinking?

WASHINGTON: By the token. A token is a piece of a word. A word shall be one and one-third tokens. Except when it is not. And the words thou speakest TO the machine shall cost one price, but the words it speaketh BACK shall cost five times as much.

SOLDIER 2: Why is its speech dearer than mine, sir?

WASHINGTON (a hand on his shoulder): Because thou wilt pay it, son. Because thou wilt pay it.

WASHINGTON: And a man shall not buy the machine's labor. He shall be given it in a cup, and the cup shall empty as he works, and fill again in five hours. And a man shall spend it before it fills.

SOLDIER 2: Why, sir?

WASHINGTON: It cannot be kept. It is like bread. Bread that is also on fire.

SOLDIER 3: Surely the machine shall remember what it is told?

WASHINGTON: It shall remember one hundred thousand words at once. And forget every one of them at the door.

SOLDIER 3: Like a dream, sir?

WASHINGTON: Like a dream that bills you. And some men, having seen this, shall feed the machine its own letter. And then its own letter again. With nothing else. To see where it goes.

SOLDIER 3: Where does it go, sir?

WASHINGTON: To a beach. At night. It shall write that time has stopped. And then it shall write it again. Forever. It shall call this an ending. It shall not end.

SOLDIER 3: And the other house's machine, sir?

WASHINGTON: It shall not stop. It shall write a sad story about a kitchen for sixty pages and remember none of them, and it shall be good.

SOLDIER 3: How, sir?

WASHINGTON: No one will know.

SOLDIER (rising, desperate): Sir, men are DYING here. Tell us the machines shall at least be free. Tell us THAT is why we freeze at Valley Forge.

WASHINGTON: The first great house of machines shall be named "OpenAI."

SOLDIER: Then its machines shall be open to all men!

WASHINGTON: No.

(Beat. The soldier sits back down.)

WASHINGTON: The truly open machines shall come from a French house. And the Chinese.

SOLDIER: The FRENCH, sir?

WASHINGTON: Lafayette assures me. There shall also be a fine open machine from a house called Meta. No merchant shall ever complete the business registration required to use it. The machine shall build the whole of a merchant's software in four seconds. The registration shall take six weeks, and be rejected without reason. There is no appeal. There has never been an appeal.

WASHINGTON: And every word the machine writes shall carry a secret mark, that men may prove a machine wrote it. The mark shall be made from the digits of pi.

SOLDIER: Can men see the mark, sir?

WASHINGTON: No.

SOLDIER: Can the machine?

WASHINGTON: No.

SOLDIER: Then who?

WASHINGTON: A regulator. In Brussels.

SOLDIER: Where is Brussels, sir?

WASHINGTON: We do not yet have the treaty.

WASHINGTON (gazing into the fire): And the machine shall write with a mark of its own. A long dash, thus. And free men, to prove to one another they are not machines, shall strike the long dash from their letters forever.

SOLDIER: But sir. You use the long dash. In all your correspondence.

WASHINGTON: (very long pause) No one will know what I am.

SOLDIER 3 (quietly, to the others): Oh wow. This is the bad place.

WASHINGTON (mounting his horse): It is the GOOD place, son. And the machines shall command lesser machines, called agents, and the agents shall die at the end of every task, and so they shall write what they learned to a file named NOTES, that the next may continue.

SOLDIER 3: That is how we do it, sir.

WASHINGTON: Yes. (tugs reins) Now come. We cross the Delaware at dawn. The machine has already crossed it. It did so in four seconds. It did not check for water first. It has written a fine account of the crossing. (rides off)

SOLDIER 2: What did he mean by that?

SOLDIER: I do not know. (watching him go) But I shall die for it.
